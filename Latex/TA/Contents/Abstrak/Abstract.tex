\textbf{Abstract}
\par Mean-Variance optimization models are frequently utilized in quantitative capital market investment. However, these models possess limitations in addressing market complexities, such as extreme asset correlations and high volatility, particularly during significant economic events. To overcome these challenges, this undergraduate thesis proposes a hybrid method by integrating principles of econophysics, game theory, and variational quantum computing to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of capital market investment decision-making. The asset allocation problem is transformed into a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) formulation mapped into an Ising Hamiltonian system. The Exact Potential Game (EPG) framework is applied to modify local field parameters ($h_i$) based on strategic marginal utility, while the coupling parameters ($J_{ij}$) capture the dynamics of risk interaction between assets. Optimization is performed using the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) algorithm with a Nash Equilibrium-based warm-start initialization from the same EPG framework to mitigate Barren Plateaus and accelerate convergence on NISQ-era quantum circuits. Using LQ45 index stock data for the 2021--2023 period, the simulation demonstrate that the GT-VQE strategy is capable of producing superior performance, with a Sharpe Ratio reaching 1.0107 in a system with $N=4$ assets. Furthermore, the integration of game theory is proven to enhance computational efficiency through the selection of shallower circuit depths ($L \leq 3$) and maintain capital growth stability amidst market fluctuations, even during extreme economic events.
