ARROW-PRATT MEASURES OF RISK AVERSION:

THE MULTIVARIATE CASE*

BY HAIM LEVY AND AZRIEL LEVY1

Arrow-Pratt measures of risk aversion have been defined for the univariate

case. For utility functions having the same ordinal preferences, we extend

Arrow's probability premium index to the multivariate case and obtain a

unique solution which can be employed to risk-aversion comparison analysis.

We also extend Duncan's definition of the risk premium vector and show that

it can be employed in comparative risk aversion once we confine ourselves to

the same preference ordering. Hence, we end up with two multivariate risk

indexes which are parallel to the Arrow and Pratt univariate indexes.

1. INTRODUCTION

Arrow (1971) and Pratt (1964) have developed the theory of risk aversion for the

univariate utility function V(x). Moreover, they compare the degree of risk

aversion which characterizes two distinct utility functions V1 (x) and V2 (x). The

extension of the Arrow-Pratt theory of risk aversion to the multivariate case raises

certain issues that do not exist in the univariate case. In particular, any comparison

of the degree of risk aversion of two multivariate utility functions u l(x I, ... , xn)

and u2(x1, ... , xn) (where x is a vector of various commodities) is impossible

unless some constraints are imposed on the utility functions under consideration.

Kihlstrom and Mirman (1974) argue that a prerequisite for the comparison of

attitudes towards risk is that the cardinal utilities being compared represent the

same ordinal preferences. Nevertheless, even for the same preference ordering, the

multivariate risk premium, as Duncan (1977) points out, is not unique, which

further complicates the comparison of the risk premium of different individuals.

Extending Pratt's approximation of the univariate risk premium, Duncan (1977)

develops a multivariate risk aversion matrix and reveals its relation to an approx-

imate (in the "small") multivariate risk premium. This derivation holds for any

utility function. Namely, Duncan does not confine himself to the same preference

ordering; hence, a risk aversion comparison analysis is meaningless.

In this paper, we provide a generalization of Arrow's (1971) univariate gamble to

the multivariate case. In a manner analogous to Arrow's derivation we derive the

probability premium of the gamble. The advantage of this probability premium

approach is that it provides a unique solution and, therefore, an unambiguous

criterion for the comparison of the risk aversion of various individuals.

We next show that this advantage is of a technical nature only. We extend

Duncan's work and show that for utility functions which belong to the same

2. DEFINITIONS AND REVIEW

The theory of risk aversion and the application of the various risk aversion

measures to situations involving risk was initially developed by Arrow (1971) and

Pratt (1964).2 Given a twice continuously differentiable utility function V(x), they

define an absolute and a relative risk aversion measure. Arrow deals with a gamble

which is a special case of Pratt's gamble.

The extension of the theory of risk aversion to multivariate utility functions and

multivariate risk raises some difficulties that do not arise in the univariate case. In

the multivariate case, there exists an infinite set of (ordinal) preference orderings.3

Each such preference ordering, in turn, implies an infinite number of cardinal utility

functions. Kihlstrom and Mirman (1974) have observed that a prerequisite for the

comparison of attitudes towards risk is that the decision makers being compared

have identical ordinal preferences over the commodity space.4

Pursuing a somewhat different path, Duncan (1977) develops a matrix measure of

local risk aversion which is related to the multivariate risk premium. Let z be a

multivariate risk and u(x) some utility function. A risk premium vector IT(x, z) is

defined as the solution to the following equation:

4. RELATION TO THE WORK OF DUNCAN

The gamble defined in Section 3 is a special case of the general risk introduced

by Duncan, just as in the univariate case Arrow's gamble is a special case of the

general risk introduced by Pratt. However, while in the univariate case both

Arrow's probability premium and Pratt's premium depend solely on the absolute

risk aversion function, the relationship between Duncan's results and those of

Section 3 of this paper is not so obvious.

It is the purpose of this section to show this relationship. To be able to compare

the risk aversion of the two utility functions in Duncan's framework, we must limit

ourselves to utility functions belonging to the same preference ordering. We show

in Theorem 2 below that for such utility functions, one can provide economic

meaning to the term "more risk averse" in Duncan's framework. Moreover, we

show that Duncan's extension holds in the small as well as in the large.5

5. CONCLUDING REMARKS

The univariate concept of probability risk premium has been extended to the

multivariate case, for utility functions belonging to the same preference ordering.

This analysis constitutes a generalization of Arrow's univariate probability risk

premium. We prove that if and only if the probability risk premium corresponding

to utility function u 1 ( x) is greater than the probability risk premium corresponding

to u2 (x) for all x, then there exists a monotonically increasing and concave

transformation k such that u 1 ( x) = k(u 2 (X)). These results are independent of the

chosen gamble vector h, which could be "small" or "large."

For utility functions which belong to the same preference ordering, we circum-

vent the nonuniqueness of the vector of risk premiums and show that Duncan's

framework can be employed in the comparison of the risk aversion of two agents.

Thus, we establish a criterion which enables comparative risk aversion in the Pratt

and Duncan framework as well.
